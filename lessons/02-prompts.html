The 4 Parts

What Makes a Good Prompt

🎭
Role

Who should AI be?

Give AI an identity. It changes how it thinks and responds. An expert gives expert answers.

"You are a senior copywriter with 15 years of experience in tech startups..."
🎯
Task

What do you need?

Be specific. "Write an email" is vague. "Write a follow-up email to a prospect who went silent" is clear.

"Write a 3-paragraph follow-up email to re-engage a prospect who hasn't responded in 2 weeks..."
📐
Format

How should it look?

Tell AI exactly what format you want. Bullets? Paragraphs? Table? Length? Be explicit.

"Format as: Subject line, opening hook, value reminder, soft CTA. Keep under 150 words."
🧩
Context

What else matters?

Background info, constraints, audience, tone. The more context, the better the output.

"Audience: B2B SaaS founders. Tone: Professional but warm. Avoid: Pushy sales language."
In Practice

Before vs After

❌ Weak Prompt

"Write me an email to a customer"

✓ Strong Prompt

"You're a customer success manager. Write a check-in email to a customer who just completed onboarding. Warm and helpful tone. Include: congratulations, one tip for getting more value, and an open invitation to ask questions. 100 words max."

Quick Checklist
Did I give AI a role or expertise level?
Is my task specific enough?
Did I specify the format/length?
Did I include relevant context?
Key Takeaway

Vague prompts get vague results.
Specific prompts get exactly what you need.
